Think of tokens and cookies like a digital ID card and a wallet. They work together to keep you logged into a website so you do not have to type your password every single time you click a link.

1. What is a Token? (The ID Card):
A Token (usually a JSON Web Token or JWT) is a highly secure, encrypted string of text generated by the server. It contains encoded information about you, such as your userId or your username.
The Analogy: It is like a concert wristband. Once the security guard (the server) checks your ticket (your password) at the gate, they give you a wristband. Every time you want to buy a drink or enter a new zone, you just show the wristband.
Access Token: A short-lived token (lasts 15 minutes to 1 hour) used to access protected data.
Refresh Token: A long-lived token (lasts days or weeks) stored in the database to automatically generate a new access token when the old one expires.


2. What is a Cookie? (The Wallet):
A Cookie is not data itself; it is a storage mechanism built directly into your web browser (Chrome, Safari, etc.). It is a small text file that a website asks your browser to save on your computer.
The Analogy: It is like your physical wallet. The wallet does not prove who you are, but it holds your ID card (the token).
Automatic Transmission: The best thing about cookies is that once a website sets one, your browser automatically attaches it to every single network request made to that website in the future.

How They Work Together (The Workflow):

Login: You send your username and password to the server.
Generation: The server verifies your password and generates an Access Token and a Refresh Token.
Delivery: The server places these tokens inside a Cookie and sends it back to your browser.
Storage: Your browser automatically saves that cookie.
Browsing: When you click "View Profile", the browser automatically sends the cookie (carrying the token inside it) back to the server. 
The server reads the token and gives you your profile data.
